# 304 vs 316 Stainless Steel Camlock Couplings: Which One for Your Line?

Both look identical. Both say "stainless steel" on the box. But 304 and 316 are different alloys with a different job, and picking the wrong one on a chemical or marine line is how couplings pit, corrode and seize.

## The difference in one table

| Property | 304 stainless | 316 stainless |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Chromium | 18% | 16-18% |
| Nickel | 8% | 10-14% |
| Molybdenum | 0% | **2-3%** |
| Chloride/salt tolerance | Good | **Excellent** |
| Acids (sulfuric, phosphoric) | Limited | Better |
| Marine/saltwater duty | Pits over time | Standard choice |
| Magnetic response | Slightly magnetic (cold-worked) | Mostly non-magnetic |
| Typical use | Water, food, general | Chemical, marine, coastal, aggressive washdown |
| Cost premium | — | ~20-40% more |

## What the molybdenum does

The 2-3% molybdenum in 316 is the entire story. It stabilizes the alloy against chloride attack — pitting and crevice corrosion from saltwater, brine, bleach and many industrial acids. 304 handles clean water and food lines for decades; put it on a saltwater deck or a bleach CIP line and it pits, starting around gasket grooves and cam-arm pivots where moisture sits.

## When 304 is fine

- Clean water, potable water, general transfer
- Food and beverage processing (304 is the food-industry standard)
- Indoor, dry, non-chloride duty
- Cost-sensitive applications where the line will not see salt or aggressive chemicals

## When to step up to 316

- **Marine and coastal**: saltwater contact, or salt air on a dock or ship deck
- **Chemical transfer**: sulfuric, phosphoric and organic acids, chloride-bearing chemicals
- **Bleach / CIP washdown**: chlorinated cleaning agents attack 304 quickly
- **Pharmaceutical and high-purity**: 316 with appropriate finish is the pharma default
- **Hot chloride service**: temperature multiplies chloride attack

## The price question

316 costs roughly 20-40% more than 304 at the same size and type. On a camlock that is a few dollars per unit — the premium buys a coupling that survives a decade on the duty it was chosen for. The expensive mistake is the opposite: saving 30% on 316 and paying for repeated replacement on a chloride line.

## Quick rule

Water, food, dry duty → 304. Salt, chemicals, bleach, marine, coastal → 316. If the line can taste salt or see bleach, do not argue with the material choice — specify 316.
