# Camlock Coupling Types: A Complete Guide to A, B, C, D, E, F, DC and DP

*Cam and groove couplings look identical at a glance — until you order the wrong letter. Type A through DP aren't sizes or materials; each letter defines what's on the non-cam end of the fitting. This guide breaks down every standard camlock type, what it connects to, and how to order the right one the first time.*

## What Is a Camlock Coupling?

A camlock coupling is a two-part, tool-free quick-connect fitting for hoses and pipes. It's also called a cam and groove coupling, a cam lock fitting, or a Kamlok-style coupling. One half is the **male adapter** (the grooved plug), the other is the **female coupler** (the socket with two pivoting cam arms). Push the adapter into the coupler and close both arms: the cams catch the groove and pull the halves together, compressing the gasket into a leak-proof seal. The whole connection takes about three seconds.

No threads, no tools, no cross-threading risk. That's why camlocks are the most widely used quick-disconnect coupling in the world for agriculture, chemical transfer, fuel delivery, dewatering, food processing and marine applications.

## How the Type System Works

Every camlock type is a combination of two ends:

1. **The cam end** — male (adapter: A, E, F, DP) or female (coupler: B, C, D, DC). Any male type mates with any female type of the same nominal size.
2. **The connection end** — male NPT thread, female NPT thread, hose shank (barb), or closed end.

The letter tells you both. Once that clicks, the system stops being confusing.

## Camlock Coupling Types Chart

| Type | Cam End | Connection End | Typical Use |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| **Type A** | Male adapter | Female NPT thread | Screws onto a male-threaded pump outlet or pipe nipple, presents a male cam end |
| **Type B** | Female coupler | Male NPT thread | Screws into a female-threaded equipment port, presents a female cam socket |
| **Type C** | Female coupler | Hose shank (barb) | Clamps onto flexible hose — the most common hose-end coupler |
| **Type D** | Female coupler | Female NPT thread | Screws onto male-threaded pipe or tank outlet, presents a female cam socket |
| **Type E** | Male adapter | Hose shank (barb) | Clamps onto flexible hose — the most common hose-end adapter |
| **Type F** | Male adapter | Male NPT thread | Screws into female-threaded ports — the rarest of the six |
| **Type DC** | Female coupler | Closed end | Dust cap: fits over a male adapter to keep it clean |
| **Type DP** | Male adapter | Closed end | Dust plug: fits into a female coupler to keep it clean |

*Thread standards: NPT is standard in North America; BSPT is common in UK/Europe/Asia. Always confirm thread type with your supplier — a 2" NPT camlock and a 2" BSPT camlock are not interchangeable.*

## Which Camlock Types Connect Together?

The cam interface is universal across manufacturers, but the gender must match. **Male adapters (A, E, F) mate with female couplers (B, C, D, DC) of the same size.** Dust caps (DC) fit *over* male adapters; dust plugs (DP) fit *into* female couplers.

The most common pairings in real systems:

| Setup | Pairing |
|:--|:--|
| Pump outlet (male thread) → hose | **Type A on pump + Type C on hose** — the workhorse combination |
| Tank/vessel outlet (male thread) → hose | **Type D on tank + Type E on hose** |
| Threaded equipment port (female thread) → hose | **Type B on port + Type C on hose** |
| Hose-to-hose joining | **Type E on one hose + Type C on the other** |
| Capping unused ends | **Type DC dust cap** on adapters, **Type DP dust plug** in couplers |

**Pro tip:** A 2" Type A from one manufacturer seals perfectly with a 2" Type D from another — as long as both are made to the same standard. This interchangeability is the point of the system.

## DC and DP: The Protective Pair

Type DC (dust cap) and Type DP (dust plug) do one job: protect disconnected ends from dirt, debris and insects that would cause leaks on the next connection.

- **DC dust cap** — female coupler with a closed end. Snaps over a Type A, E or F adapter.
- **DP dust plug** — male adapter with a solid closed end. Plugs into a Type B, C or D coupler.

⚠️ **DC and DP are NOT pressure-rated.** They seal open ends for storage and transport only. For pressure-testing a closed line, use a threaded blanking plug behind a Type B or D coupler.

## Materials: Aluminum, Stainless Steel, Brass, Polypropylene

Material choice drives pressure rating, chemical compatibility and price:

| Material | Strength | Best For | Working Pressure (¾"–2") |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| **Aluminum** | Light, good balance of cost/performance | Water transfer, fuel, general industrial | 250 psi |
| **Stainless Steel 316** | Highest corrosion resistance, hygienic | Chemical, food & beverage, pharmaceutical, marine | 250 psi |
| **Brass** | Good machinability, marine-grade options | Water, fuel, general industrial | 250 psi |
| **Polypropylene** | Chemically inert, cheapest, light | Agricultural chemicals, water, drainage | 100 psi |

**⚠️ Never mix materials in one connection.** A polypropylene coupler on an aluminum adapter can fail under pressure because of the tensile strength difference. Use the same material on both halves.

### 304 vs 316 Stainless Steel

304 (18% chromium, 8% nickel) handles less aggressive environments. 316 adds 2–3% molybdenum for superior resistance to chlorides, acids and salt — the right choice for marine, pharmaceutical and food-contact service. For most industrial camlock applications, 316 is the standard buyers specify.

## Pressure Ratings at a Glance

Camlock couplings are low-to-medium pressure fittings. Working pressure drops as size increases and as temperature rises (derating applies above ambient with elastomeric gaskets; polypropylene's max working temperature is 158°F, above which ratings drop by a further 40%).

| Material | ½" | ¾"–2" | 2½" | 3" | 4" | 5"–6" |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Brass | 150 psi | 250 psi | 150 psi | 125 psi | 100 psi | 75 psi |
| Aluminum | 150 psi | 250 psi | 150 psi | 125 psi | 100 psi | 75 psi |
| Stainless 316 | 150 psi | 250 psi | 225 psi | 200 psi | 100 psi | 100 psi |
| Polypropylene | 75 psi | 100 psi | — | 50 psi | — | — |

*Gasket type matters too: standard elastomeric (Buna-N/EPDM) ratings above; PTFE gaskets reduce pressure ratings.*

## Standards: Why Any Camlock Fits Any Camlock

Camlocks are interchangeable because they're built to published specifications:

- **A-A-59326** (US federal, formerly MIL-C-27487) — dimensions, tolerances, materials, pressure ratings, casting methods and inspection procedures
- **EN 14420-7 / DIN 2828** (Europe) — hose fittings with cam locking system

Buy from a supplier who states compliance openly. That's your guarantee that a coupler from one maker accepts an adapter from another.

## How to Choose the Right Camlock Coupling

1. **Measure the hose bore, not the outside.** Camlock sizes refer to nominal hose/pipe bore (e.g., a 2" camlock clamps onto 2" hose). A 2" coupler will NOT accept a 1½" adapter.
2. **Pick the connection end.** Hose? Type C or E (hose shank). Male thread? Type A or D. Female thread? Type B or F.
3. **Match the material to the media.** Chemicals → SS316 or PP. Food → SS316. Water/fuel → aluminum or brass.
4. **Verify pressure and temperature.** Your system's max working pressure (including spikes) must stay under the rating at your operating temperature.
5. **Confirm thread standard.** NPT vs BSPT — get it wrong and nothing threads on.
6. **Always pair male-to-female.** Male adapters mate with female couplers only, same size.

## FAQ

**Can I connect camlocks from different manufacturers?**
Yes — if both halves are made to A-A-59326 / EN 14420-7, they're dimensionally interchangeable regardless of brand.

**Why is my camlock leaking at the connection?**
Check the gasket is present, clean and compatible with your fluid. Make sure the adapter is fully seated and both arms are closed evenly.

**Can I mix camlock materials?**
Sometimes, but the assembly is limited by its least compatible material and rating. Safest rule: same material on both halves.

**Are DC/DP caps pressure-rated?**
No. They protect disconnected ends from contamination — they are not for capping pressurised lines.

**Can camlocks be used for compressed air or steam?**
**No.** Cam and groove couplings must not be used for compressed air, gas or steam service.

**Do I need safety pins?**
Female couplers come standard with safety pins that lock the handles. They're not needed under normal conditions but add security on vibration-prone or high-value connections.
