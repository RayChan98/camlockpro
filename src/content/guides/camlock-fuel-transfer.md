# Camlock Couplings for Fuel Transfer: Diesel, Gasoline and Lubricants

Camlock couplings are the workhorse quick-connect on fuel transfer lines — tanker trucks, bulk storage, generator refueling skids and machinery service carts all use them. But fuel is not water: the wrong material or the wrong gasket will degrade, harden or fail inside a fuel line. Here is how to spec camlocks for diesel, gasoline and lubricant duty.

## Material rules for fuel

| Fuel | Recommended material | Why |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Diesel / heating oil | **Aluminum** | Light, economical, corrosion-resistant in diesel; the industry standard for diesel transfer rigs |
| Gasoline | **Brass** (or stainless) | Brass is spark-resistant and unaffected by gasoline aromatics; aluminum is not recommended for gasoline service |
| Aviation fuel / Jet A | **Stainless steel** | Fuel standards demand full compatibility and traceability; 316 is the conservative choice |
| Lubricants / hydraulic oil | **Aluminum or brass** | Both handle oils well; match the fitting to the hose and hardware already in the system |

Mixed-material rule applies here too: **never pair dissimilar coupling metals** (e.g. aluminum body with a brass adapter on a pressure line) — galvanic corrosion and strength mismatch are both real failure modes on fuel rigs.

## The gasket is a fuel-specific decision

**EPDM is the default water gasket and it is the wrong choice for fuel.** EPDM swells and degrades in petroleum products.

- **NBR (Buna-N)** is the fuel-service standard — oil-resistant, rated for diesel, gasoline, lubricants and most mineral oils, typically -40°F to +250°F working range
- **Viton (FKM)** where higher temperature or aromatic/aggressive fuel blends are involved (biofuel blends, some aviation applications)
- **PTFE** for maximum chemical inertness, at the cost of a harder seal — used on specialty chemical fuels

For bulk fuel orders, spec **NBR gaskets and a spare set** — a hardened gasket is the most common cause of a fuel line leak after a season of service.

## Standard layouts

- **Pump to hose**: Type A (male NPT) on the pump outlet → Type C (male hose shank) into the suction/discharge hose — the classic A+C pair
- **Hose to tank / drum**: Type D (female hose shank) on the tank fill port, Type E (male hose shank, offset) where a straight shank will not reach
- **Tanker discharge**: 2" to 4" aluminum, A at the valve outlet, C into layflat or hardwall transfer hose

## Pressure reality on fuel lines

Fuel transfer is a low-to-medium pressure job. Reference values from the standard camlock rating table: **2" aluminum is rated around 200 psi working pressure** — far above the 50-150 psi typical of transfer pumps and gravity flow. As with water service, **size for flow, not pressure**: 2" for up to roughly 150 gpm, 3" to ~300 gpm, 4" for bulk loading rates.

## Safety checklist for fuel duty

1. **Bonding and grounding** — camlocks are metal, but the whole transfer system (truck, hose, receiving tank) must be bonded and grounded before flow starts. The coupling does not replace grounding.
2. **Never use camlocks for pressurized gas, LPG or steam** — cam and groove couplings are not rated for gas service.
3. **Release pressure before uncoupling** — bleed the line, then open the arms. A pressurized fuel line that comes apart sprays fuel and creates an ignition risk.
4. **Inspect gaskets monthly on fuel rigs** — fuel degrades rubber faster than water; look for swelling, cracks and hardness.
5. **Cap open ends** — dust caps and plugs keep grit out of the groove; a fuel line with a gritty seal leaks and drips.

## Checklist for a fuel transfer order

- Sizes: match the hose bore (1½", 2", 3", 4")
- Types: A + C (pump to hose), D + E (hose to tank) as the layout requires
- Material: aluminum (diesel), brass (gasoline), stainless (aviation/specialty)
- Gasket: **NBR** (standard fuel), Viton (high-temp/aggressive blends)
- Extras: dust caps and plugs, spare gaskets
