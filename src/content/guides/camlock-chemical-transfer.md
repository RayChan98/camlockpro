# Camlock Couplings for Chemical Transfer: Materials, Gaskets and Safety

Chemical transfer lines are where camlock selection stops being a price exercise. The coupling is a pressure vessel at the end of a hose, and the two parts that decide whether it survives are the body material and the gasket compound.

## Body material first

| Media type | Recommended body | Why |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Strong acids, aggressive solvents | **Polypropylene** or **316 stainless** | PP resists most acids/solvents at moderate temperature; 316 SS adds heat and strength |
| Caustics, alkalis | **Polypropylene** or **316 SS** | Aluminum fails fast on caustics |
| General industrial chemicals | **316 stainless** | Corrosion resistance with mechanical strength |
| Not recommended | **Aluminum** (attacked by acids/alkalis), **brass** (ammonia, some acids) | Galvanic and direct chemical attack |

Temperature matters: polypropylene camlocks are typically rated to about 65-80°C (149-176°F) continuous. Above that, move to stainless steel. Check the specific pressure rating at your service temperature — PP ratings derate as temperature rises.

## Gasket compound

- **Viton (FKM)**: the workhorse for chemical lines — broad resistance to acids, solvents and hydrocarbons, rated to ~200°C
- **PTFE**: nearly universal inertness and the best high-temperature option, but harder; specify an encapsulated or spring-energized design for pressure service
- **EPDM**: good for some chemicals and hot water, but attacked by hydrocarbons — not a chemical-line default
- **NBR**: oil and fuel, not general chemicals

When in doubt on a chemical line: 316 stainless body + Viton gasket is the safe default. Polypropylene + Viton covers moderate-temperature acids at lower cost.

## Safety rules for chemical loading lines

1. **Pressure-test the assembly before each connection** — a coupling failure on a chemical line is not a nuisance, it is an exposure incident.
2. **Use dust caps and plugs** on open ends. They keep contamination out and residual product in, and they stop foreign objects damaging the sealing face.
3. **Ground the line for flammable transfer.** Camlock couplings are conductive in aluminum/stainless, but polypropylene is not — an ungrounded PP line can build static charge on solvent transfer. Fit a ground strap or use conductive hardware.
4. **Never mix materials between halves** on aggressive media — galvanic corrosion between an aluminum adapter and stainless coupler accelerates in chemical environments.
5. **Replace gaskets on schedule**, not on failure. Chemical exposure hardens compounds even when they look intact.
6. **Match the thread standard on pipe connections** (NPT vs BSPT) — a poor thread seal leaks under chemical pressure before the camlock ever does.

## Spec checklist for a chemical camlock line

- Media, concentration and temperature
- Body material (PP for moderate acids/solvents, 316 SS for heat/strength/caustics)
- Gasket compound (Viton default, PTFE for extreme service)
- Size (nominal bore) and type (A-F, DC, DP)
- Thread standard on pipe ends
- Grounding plan for flammable media
- Spare gaskets and dust caps in stock
