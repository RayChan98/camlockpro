# Camlock Couplings for Irrigation: Aluminum Couplings on Pivots, Drip and Flood Lines

Walk any irrigated farm and you will see camlocks. They are the standard quick-connect on irrigation water lines because they are fast, cheap and tough enough for field life. Aluminum is the default material; here is how to spec them properly for ag duty.

## Why aluminum

- **Cost**: aluminum camlocks are the least expensive full-metal option, and irrigation does not need stainless
- **Weight**: a 3" aluminum coupler weighs a fraction of a steel one — crews move them all day
- **Corrosion**: aluminum handles clean water and most fertilizer solutions at field temperatures; anodized finishes add life
- **Availability**: sizes ½" to 6" across types A-F, DC and DP are stocked everywhere

## Where camlocks are used on an irrigation system

- **Pump discharge**: coupling the pump outlet to layflat or PVC mainline — the most common single point
- **Center pivots**: connecting sections and the water supply line
- **Drip irrigation headers**: mainline-to-submain and flush connections
- **Flood/levee irrigation**: laying and shifting lines across paddocks
- **Fertilizer injection**: mixing tanks and chemigation lines (use PP or SS here if fertigation chemicals are aggressive)

## Sizing and pressure

Irrigation mains typically run 30-100 psi. A 2" aluminum camlock is rated well above that (the standard rating table gives 2" aluminum about 200 psi working pressure), so sizing is about **flow, not pressure**. Rule of thumb: 2" for up to ~150 gpm, 3" to ~300 gpm, 4" for larger mains. Match the coupling size to the hose bore, not the pump outlet thread.

## Gaskets for field duty

- **EPDM** handles water and general ag duty with a long field life — the default
- **NBR** for fuel/lubricant transfer around machinery, not for plain water lines
- **Viton/PTFE** only for chemigation or aggressive fertigation — unnecessary cost on plain water
- Keep **spare gaskets** with the pump; field gaskets get sun, grit and abuse

## Field installation tips

1. **Dust caps on every open end** — grit in the groove destroys gaskets within a season. This is the single cheapest upgrade for an irrigation setup.
2. **Support long hose runs** — camlock arms are not designed to carry hose weight at the connection. Support the hose so the coupling is not a hinge.
3. **Close both arms fully.** Half-closed arms on a 100 psi line will blow off — usually at the far end of the paddock.
4. **Rinse after fertigation** — fertilizer residue accelerates corrosion on aluminum bodies and hardens gaskets.
5. **Anodized aluminum for permanent installs** — bare castings pit faster in wet, alkaline soils.

## Checklist for an irrigation order

- Sizes: nominal hose bore (2", 3", 4")
- Types: A (male threaded), B (female threaded), C (male hose shank), D (female hose shank), E (male hose shank, offset), F (female, offset) as your pump and pipe connections demand
- Material: aluminum (default), polypropylene (chemigation), stainless (permanent corrosive duty)
- Gasket: EPDM
- Extras: dust caps and plugs, gaskets in bulk, anodized finish for permanent installs
