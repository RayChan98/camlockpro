# Camlock Couplings for Fire Department Water Supply and Tanker Fill

Walk a fire station bay and you will see camlocks — on dump tanks, pump intakes, wildland water bladders and fill lines. They are the standard quick-connect for the **water supply side** of fireground operations. One clarification up front: fire hose itself uses storz or NH threads, not camlock. Camlock's job in fire service is the high-flow supply plumbing — moving water between tankers, dump tanks, pumps and portable bladders.

## The division of labor on the fireground

- **Fire hose** → storz (European-style bayonet) or NH (national standard thread) — the hose couplings stay on those standards
- **Dump tank / portable water tank** → camlock on fill and suction ports
- **Tanker-to-dump-tank fill** → camlock on the discharge and fill lines
- **Wildland bladders and drop tanks** → camlock on the fill/dump ports
- **Pump supply** → camlock where the hard suction or supply hose meets the pump intake

## Why aluminum dominates fire-service camlocks

- **Weight**: crews move and connect these in turnout gear; a 4" aluminum coupling weighs a fraction of brass or stainless
- **Speed**: cam and groove connects in seconds without tools — critical when a tanker is waiting
- **Corrosion**: aluminum handles clean water, and the occasional muddy fill, better than bare steel
- **Cost**: departments replace and expand supply plumbing in quantity; aluminum keeps the budget sane

Stainless appears on **foam concentrate** systems: foam concentrates are aggressive, so PP or stainless bodies with **Viton gaskets** are the rule on foam plumbing. Plain-water supply stays aluminum + EPDM.

## Sizing: fire service is high-flow

Fireground water moves fast — the goal is filling a dump tank or bladder in minutes:

- **3"** — standard tanker fill and pump supply lines
- **4"** — high-volume tanker fill, large pumpers
- **5"-6"** — mass water movement, large dump tanks, wildland supply

Reference pressure note: fire supply is moderate pressure (50-150 psi typical at the pump). Camlocks are rated far above that (2" aluminum ≈ 200 psi working; 4" is lower but still comfortable for supply duty), so sizing is about **flow volume, not pressure**. Match the coupling to the hose or port bore.

## Typical fire-service layouts

- **Dump tank fill**: Type A (male NPT) on the tanker discharge → Type C (male hose shank) into the fill hose → Type D (female hose shank) on the dump tank port
- **Pump intake**: Type D on the hard suction or supply hose, mated to the pump intake adapter
- **Wildland bladder**: Type C or D on the bladder port; a 3" camlock is the common wildland fill standard
- **Washdown / station utility**: 1"-1½" aluminum for apparatus washdown and station water

## Fire-service safety notes

1. **Verify full arm closure before flow** — a half-closed camlock on a 4" fill line is a violent failure waiting to happen; crews check both arms every connection
2. **Dust caps on open ports** — dump tank ports left open collect gravel and grit; a scored groove ruins the seal
3. **No camlock on foam lines without Viton** — foam concentrate destroys EPDM gaskets
4. **Grounding on fuel transfer** — camlocks on tanker fuel connections require bonding/grounding just like any fuel rig
5. **Inspect gaskets seasonally** — UV and ozone age EPDM; a hardened gasket on a supply line leaks under full flow

## Checklist for a fire-service order

- Sizes: 3" and 4" (supply/fill), 1"-1½" (utility), 5"-6" (mass water)
- Types: A + C (tanker discharge to hose), D (tank/port connections)
- Material: aluminum (water supply), stainless or PP (foam concentrate), 316 for marine-adjacent stations
- Gasket: EPDM (water), Viton (foam)
- Extras: dust caps and plugs in quantity, spare gaskets, anodized aluminum for permanent station installs
