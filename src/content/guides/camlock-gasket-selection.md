# Camlock Gasket Selection Guide: EPDM, NBR, Viton or PTFE?

The gasket is the only part of a camlock that actually seals. The body holds the parts together; the gasket stops the leak. Pick the wrong compound and the coupling fails even though the metalwork is perfect.

## Gasket compound quick table

| Compound | Media | Temperature range | Cost | Notes |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| **EPDM** | Water, steam, mild chemicals, brake fluid | -40°C to 120°C (-40°F to 248°F) | $ | The default for water and general duty. Poor resistance to oils, fuels, solvents |
| **NBR (Buna-N)** | Oil, fuel, hydraulic fluid, lubricants | -30°C to 100°C (-22°F to 212°F) | $ | The default for fuel and oil transfer |
| **Viton (FKM)** | Aggressive chemicals, acids, solvents, high temperature | -20°C to 200°C (-4°F to 392°F) | $$$ | Broad chemical resistance; the premium choice for chemical lines |
| **PTFE** | Nearly everything, high temperature | -200°C to 260°C (-328°F to 500°F) | $$$ | Inert, but harder — seals best with an outer elastomer ring or careful torquing |

## How to choose

1. **Identify the media.** Water → EPDM. Fuel, diesel, oil → NBR. Solvents, acids, aggressive chemicals → Viton or PTFE. Steam → EPDM (steam) or PTFE (hot process).
2. **Check temperature.** A gasket that handles the chemical but not the temperature is still a failure. EPDM handles hot water and steam; NBR softens above ~100°C; Viton handles 200°C.
3. **Check the other components.** The gasket must suit the coupling material too — a polypropylene body with a Viton gasket carrying a chemical that attacks polypropylene still fails. Match the whole assembly to the media.
4. **Food contact?** Specify food-grade (FDA/NSF) gaskets for food and beverage lines. Standard EPDM and NBR compounds are not all food-safe.

## The cost trap

Viton and PTFE gaskets cost several times more than EPDM or NBR — worth it on a chemical line, wasted on a water line. Conversely, running EPDM on a fuel line saves a few cents per gasket and costs a tank of product per leak. The gasket is the cheapest part of the connection and the one that decides whether it works.

## How to change a camlock gasket

1. Remove the old gasket from the machined groove in the coupler face — it usually peels out; a pick helps if it is perished.
2. Clean the groove — grit under the new gasket re-introduces the leak.
3. Seat the new gasket evenly in the groove, flat and untwisted.
4. Re-connect and pressure-test. A gasket change takes under a minute on an open line.

## When to replace

- Flattened, cracked or hardened gaskets (feel: it should stay flexible)
- After heavy chemical exposure, even if it looks fine
- After the coupling has been apart several times
- Gaskets are consumables. Stock spares for every size you run.
