# NPT vs BSPT vs BSPP Threads on Camlock Fittings: The Complete Guide

The camlock body is rarely the leak. The thread connecting it to the pipe is. NPT, BSPT and BSPP look similar at arm's length, and a mismatched thread will leak, cross-thread or fail under pressure — often months later, after the system is buried.

## The three standards

| Standard | Taper? | Sealing method | Where used |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| **NPT** (National Pipe Taper) | Taper | Threads wedge + sealant | North America |
| **BSPT** (British Standard Pipe Taper) | Taper | Threads wedge + sealant | UK, Europe, Asia, most of the world |
| **BSPP** (British Standard Pipe Parallel) | Parallel | Gasket / O-ring / bonded washer | UK, Europe, hydraulic systems |

## NPT vs BSPT: the same idea, different angles

Both are 55° vs 60° thread forms... no — that's the common confusion. NPT has a **60° thread angle**; BSPT has a **55° thread angle**. NPT and BSPT are both tapered (about 1°47' per side... the taper is 3/4" per foot for NPT, 1 in 16 for BSP — effectively the same taper), but the different thread angle means **they do not seal with each other**. An NPT male into a BSPT female can start, feel tight, and leak — or cross-thread under torque.

Pitch also differs: NPT is measured in threads per inch (11.5 TPI at 1", 8 TPI at 2"), BSPT is also measured in TPI but at different values (11 TPI at 1", 11 TPI at 2"). Close, but not interchangeable.

## How to tell them apart

1. **Measure the angle**: NPT = 60°, BSPT = 55°. A thread gauge settles it instantly.
2. **Measure the pitch**: 1" NPT = 11.5 TPI, 1" BSPT = 11 TPI. Count threads over an inch.
3. **Check the context**: American-made valves and North American plumbing → NPT. European/Asian equipment, compressors, hydraulics → BSP.

## BSPP: the parallel one

BSPP is parallel and seals with a **gasket or O-ring**, not on the threads. A parallel thread into a taper thread will not wedge — it needs the sealing washer. Mixing BSPP male into BSPT female strips the seal and leaks at low pressure.

## The camlock connection

On a camlock adapter (types A, B, C, D), the thread standard is cast into the part's spec. Ordering the wrong one is the second most common camlock mistake (after wrong size):

- **NPT camlocks** for North American pipe systems
- **BSPT camlocks** for European/Asian pipe systems  
- **BSPP camlocks** with O-ring for parallel-thread hydraulic/compressor connections

## How to avoid the mismatch

1. **State the thread standard on every order** — never assume "standard threads"
2. **Check both sides of the connection** — the pipe/fitting side and the camlock side must match
3. **Use adapters for mixed systems** — NPT-to-BSPT adapters exist; fit them rather than forcing threads
4. **Test-fit before pressurizing** — a thread that starts and stops in the first two turns is probably the wrong standard
